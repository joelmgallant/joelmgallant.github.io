The Vision

The veil lifts on the 1600s, in Mi'kma'ki, the land the Mi'kmaq have always called home, where forest meets shore along these very coasts. The Acadians and the Mi'kmaq lived side by side there as allies, friends, and often family. That bond is real in history, not only in the stars.

He was an Acadian builder in a cabin among the trees, working cedar and spruce with patient, rough hands. He kept the chimney smoking in case the people he loved came home.

She was a Mi'kmaw woman by the water, a storyteller whose hands danced as she spoke. People gathered to hear her, and she helped them grow stronger and wiser.

He was new to the land, learning to survive it. She already knew its ways. So she taught him, and he built beside her people, and a friendship formed that outlasted the seasons.

The Long Dark

Then came the hard years, the Expulsion of 1755, when so many Acadians were torn from their homes. His deepest fear came true. History tells us the Mi'kmaq sheltered Acadians who fled into the woods, and in this vision she was there, keeping him hidden and steady through the long dark.

Full Circle

In this life, the debt and the love come full circle. He still builds, teaches, and leads. She still lifts people up. The stones you've swapped carry the old promise forward: each of you guarding what the other can't carry alone.

Old friends, across centuries, found again on the same shores.

🖤 The Wrist-Switching Ritual

The onyx changes its current depending on the wrist. On Joel's left wrist, the receiving side, it draws in calm and lets him set his own weight down. On his right wrist, the giving side, it sends Saturn's steadiness and protection outward, straight to Nancy, shielding her the way she holds his fire.

Remember the Six of Pentacles: Joel's lesson is learning to receive, not only to give. So switch wrists with the days. Left when he needs grounding for himself, right when he wants to send his strength to Nancy. The energy flows both ways, like a tide between the cabin and the shore.
